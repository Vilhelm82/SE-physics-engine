# Reflection loop: composite control with finite sign ramps

**Derived:** a real pulse sequence cancels the common first-order gain error of every exact-return reflection loop. Leakage becomes quartic; the remaining logical phase is quadratic. The complete response distinguishes gain drift, hold calibration, reference phase and bright loss, and gives a quantitative boundary for using the composite.

At the first return and a common **+1% gain error**, the exact propagators give leakage **0.00106146 → 0.000000321669** and average logical infidelity **0.000530776 → 0.000000249482**. With each sign ramp lasting \(0.2/a\), duration increases from \(18.2510/a\) to \(57.2474/a\); nominal bright-loss exposure triples. These are predictions of the specified finite-level realization.

Inputs: native [REALFIBER path](</home/williaml/Cella Framework/Papers_Library/02_theorems_and_lemmas/geometric_fault_localization_and_decomposition/REALFIBER_THEOREM.md>); [RF-1–7](2026-09-05-reflection-loop-finite-duration.md); \(i\dot U=HU\); signed real couplings; repeatable multiplicative gain; fixed encoded reference. The sign-changing holds extend the fixed-gap path through \(H=0\), with the encoded plane retained as attached state. Loss and reference-noise assumptions are specified below.

## CP-1. Construction and universal cancellation

Write \(d=e_3\), \(r_0=e_4\), \(P=|d\rangle\langle d|+|r_0\rangle\langle r_0|\), and \(Q=I-P\). On the active triad,

\[
J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\1&0&0\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
R=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\1&0&0\\0&0&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The original loop \(F_\epsilon\), with actual Hamiltonian \((1+\epsilon)H_f(t)\), has nominal endpoint \(F_0=R\oplus1\) at

\[
\tau_n=\frac{\pi}{2a}\sqrt{16n^2-1},\quad T_n=3\tau_n,\quad
r_n=1-\frac1{16n^2},\quad b_n=\frac\pi2 r_n,\quad n\ge1.
\tag{1}
\]

The inverse is physically generated by **sign-reversed backward playback**,

\[
H_{\rm inv}(t)=-(1+\epsilon)H_f(T_n-t),\qquad U_{\rm inv}=F_\epsilon^\dagger.
\tag{2}
\]

Equation (2) holds at every gain, not just at resonance. Positive backward playback alone gives a different propagator. The same gain must apply to the corresponding forward and inverse controls; an additive detuning is not inverted by this operation.

Each endpoint hold has Hamiltonian \((1+\epsilon)q(t)J\oplus0\), area \(\int q(t)dt=\theta=\pi/3\), and propagator

\[
S_\epsilon=e^{-i(1+\epsilon)\theta J}\oplus1.
\]

Execute, in chronological order,

\[
\boxed{F_\epsilon\;\longrightarrow\;S_\epsilon\;\longrightarrow\;
F_\epsilon^\dagger\;\longrightarrow\;S_\epsilon\;\longrightarrow\;F_\epsilon.}
\tag{3}
\]

The resulting operator is \(C_\epsilon=F_\epsilon S_\epsilon F_\epsilon^\dagger S_\epsilon F_\epsilon\). Nominally \(C_0=R S_0^2\oplus1\); its logical action is still \(G=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1)\).

**Cancellation theorem.** More generally, let \(F_\epsilon=R V_\epsilon\oplus1\), with \(V_0=I\), analytic unitary \(V_\epsilon\), and \(\langle d|\dot V_0|d\rangle=0\). Suppose the implemented inverse is \(F_\epsilon^\dagger\), and both holds depend differentiably on the error and preserve \(P\) pointwise, including their errors. If their nominal bright action is \(S=e^{-i\pi J/3}\), then

\[
\left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\big(C_0^\dagger C_\epsilon P\big)\right|_0=0.
\tag{4}
\]

**Proof.** For the constructed holds, \(R^2=I\) and \(RJ=JR\) give

\[
C_0^\dagger C_\epsilon=S^{-2}V_\epsilon S_\epsilon
V_\epsilon^\dagger S_\epsilon V_\epsilon.
\]

Put \(k=\dot V_0d\in Q\mathcal H\). Hold derivatives annihilate \(d\). The derivative of this column is \(S^{-2}(I-S+S^2)k=0\), because \(J^2=I\) on the whole bright plane and \(e^{\pm i\pi/3}\) are the roots of \(z^2-z+1\). The reference column is fixed exactly. Other differentiable hold errors with \(S_{j,\epsilon}P=P\) also contribute zero to this first derivative. Thus cancellation holds for every bright leakage direction, without fitting a particular error vector. A primitive first-order logical phase would survive; the native primitive has none.

For two nominal areas \(\theta_1,\theta_2\), universal cancellation requires

\[
I-e^{i\theta_2J}+e^{i(\theta_1+\theta_2)J}=0\quad\text{on }Q.
\tag{5}
\]

Taking either eigenvalue of \(J\), the three unit complex numbers must close an equilateral triangle. Consequently \(\theta_1=\theta_2=\pi/3\) or both \(-\pi/3\), modulo \(2\pi\). The smallest nonnegative total area is \(2\pi/3\). This classifies the holds in this three-loop architecture.

## CP-2. Full leading response, including the phase

For the native primitive, \(M=iV'_0\) obeys

\[
Md=i b_n(2e_1-e_2),\qquad \langle d|M|d\rangle=0.
\]

Let \(W_\epsilon=C_0^\dagger C_\epsilon\). Direct expansion gives, for every \(n\),

\[
W_\epsilon d=d+\epsilon^2
\begin{pmatrix}
\pi^2r_n(-2\sqrt3+3i)/12\\
\pi^2r_n(\sqrt3-6i)/12\\
i\sqrt3\pi^2r_n^2/2
\end{pmatrix}
+O(\epsilon^3).
\tag{6}
\]

With \(z=-\langle d|C_\epsilon|d\rangle\), the measurable response is

\[
\begin{aligned}
p_{\rm leak}&=15b_n^2\theta^2\epsilon^4+O(\epsilon^5),\\
\arg z&=2\sqrt3\,b_n^2\epsilon^2+O(\epsilon^3),\\
1-F_{\rm av}&=\left(\frac{15}{2}b_n^2\theta^2+2b_n^4\right)
\epsilon^4+O(\epsilon^5).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

Here average fidelity includes leakage and averages uniformly over pure logical inputs. Exactly, for the unitary sequence with reference fixed,

\[
1-F_{\rm av}=\frac{p_{\rm leak}}2+\frac{|z-1|^2}{6}.
\tag{8}
\]

At \(n=1\), the coefficients of leakage, phase and infidelity are respectively \(375\pi^4/1024=35.67227455\), \(225\sqrt3\pi^2/512=7.51230012\), and \(27.24191279\). The original leakage coefficient is \(5b_1^2=10.84307124\), multiplying \(\epsilon^2\).

**Algebraic derivation of (6).** Put \(\lambda=a\tau_n\), \(q=\pi/2\), \(L_j=\lambda J+qG_j\), with \(G_1,G_2,G_1\) from RF-1. Expansion of each exact moving-frame exponential about its \(2\pi n\) return gives

\[
E_j=I-i\epsilon r_nL_j+\epsilon^2
\left[-i\left(\frac{r_n(1-3r_n)}2L_j+r_n\lambda J\right)
-\frac{r_n^2}{2}L_j^2\right]+O(\epsilon^3).
\tag{9}
\]

Multiply \(V=E_3E_2E_1\), insert \(S_\epsilon=S(I-i\epsilon\theta J-\epsilon^2\theta^2J^2/2)+O(\epsilon^3)\) into CP-1, and collect the \(d\) column. The \(\lambda\) terms cancel, leaving (6). The runner checks the matrix identity with symbolic \(r_n,\lambda\), so it covers the entire return family. Taking the bright norm and the logical phase gives (7).

### Separate loop and hold errors

With small loop gains \(\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2,\epsilon_3\), the leading leakage is

\[
\boxed{p_{\rm leak}=\frac{5b_n^2}{2}
\big[(\epsilon_1-\epsilon_2)^2+(\epsilon_2-\epsilon_3)^2+
(\epsilon_3-\epsilon_1)^2\big]+O(\|\epsilon\|^3).}
\tag{10}
\]

This follows by taking the norm of \(b_n[\epsilon_1I-\epsilon_2A+\epsilon_3A^2](2,-1)^T\), where \(A=e^{i\theta J}\) on \(Q\). The common mode vanishes. Independent zero-mean loop gains of variance \(s^2\) give leading mean leakage \(15b_n^2s^2\); cancellation requires correlation across the complete sequence.

For a common loop gain \(\epsilon\) and separate fractional hold gains \(\eta_1,\eta_2\) in chronological order, let \(v=(2,-1)^T\). The full bright term of total degree two is

\[
QW_{\epsilon,\eta}d=-i b_n\theta\epsilon J
(\eta_1I+\eta_2S)v+O((|\epsilon|+\|\eta\|)^3).
\tag{11}
\]

Its squared norm is \(5b_n^2\theta^2\epsilon^2(\eta_1^2+\eta_2^2+\eta_1\eta_2)\); the phase through degree two remains \(2\sqrt3 b_n^2\epsilon^2\). With independently exact holds, leakage is \(O(\epsilon^6)\), while average logical infidelity remains quartic because of this phase. Hold errors alone, at any area, leave the nominal logical gate exact. Equation (7) uses the physically shared-gain case \(\eta_1=\eta_2=\epsilon\).

### Variation during the waveform

For a small time-dependent gain \(g(t)\), define the exact nominal response vector

\[
y(t)=QU_0(t)^\dagger H_0(t)U_0(t)d.
\]

The first-order bright error is \(-i\int g(t)y(t)dt\), and (4) says \(\int y(t)dt=0\). For a Lipschitz gain with constant \(L\),

\[
\left\|\int g(t)y(t)dt\right\|
\le L\int |t-t_*|\,\|y(t)\|dt.
\tag{12}
\]

This follows by subtracting \(g(t_*)\int y=0\). Also \(PU_0^\dagger H_0U_0P=0\) throughout the nominal waveform, so arbitrary gain variation has no first-order projected logical Hamiltonian. It can still cause leakage. For \(n=1\), ramps \(0.2/a\), and \(g(t)=\zeta(t/T_c-1/2)\), the computed leading leakage is \(4.23373914\,\zeta^2\), independently checked by laboratory integration. Here \(\zeta\) is the total gain change from start to finish.

## CP-3. Continuous sign changes and exact resource costs

The first hold starts at \(+aJ\), stays there for \(\theta/a\), then ramps linearly to \(-aJ\) over \(\rho\). The second ramps from \(-aJ\) to \(+aJ\) over \(\rho\), then stays at \(+aJ\) for \(\theta/a\). Each ramp has zero signed area. Thus (3) is realized by a continuous real, zero-diagonal Hamiltonian, with

\[
T_c=3T_n+\frac{2\theta}{a}+2\rho.
\tag{13}
\]

At the midpoint of either ramp the gap closes. Nevertheless \(H(t)P=0\) on the entire hold, so every encoded state is exactly stationary there. The instantaneous kernel grows at \(H=0\); the retained encoded plane supplies the continuation. No isolated-band adiabatic argument is needed across that point.

For \(\lvert q\rvert\le a\), \(|\dot q|\le\nu\), a hold connecting opposite endpoints with area \(+\theta\) satisfies

\[
t_{\rm hold}\ge\frac\theta a+\frac{2a}{\nu}.
\tag{14}
\]

**Proof.** For a downward switch ending at \(-a\), \(q(t)\le\min(a,-a+\nu(t_{\rm hold}-t))\). Integration gives \(\int q\le a t_{\rm hold}-2a^2/\nu\). The upward case follows by reversing time. A plateau and maximal-slew linear ramp attain the bound. Hence choosing \(\rho=2a/\nu\) gives

\[
T_c=3T_n+2\theta/a+4a/\nu.
\tag{15}
\]

This is the minimum hold time for these endpoint/area constraints. The original arcs also require slew at least \(a\pi/(2\tau_n)\); they are not shortened by (14).

Nominally every forward and inverse traversal has zero projected energy, and every hold annihilates \(P\). The composite therefore remains a nonadiabatic holonomic reflection. Let \(v_n=1/(4n)\). Its peak instantaneous bright population and integrated exposure are

\[
p_{\max}=4v_n^2(1-v_n^2),\qquad
X_c=3X_n,\qquad X_n=T_n(2v_n^2-\tfrac32v_n^4).
\tag{16}
\]

The inverse retraces the nominal population; holds contribute zero. At the first return, \(p_{\max}=15/64\), \(aX_n=2.17443603\), and \(aX_c=6.52330809\). These are nominal exposures; gain-induced population during the holds contributes to mixed noise corrections.

## CP-4. Reference phase, loss and the decision boundary

If the physical reference has an unknown static frequency offset \(\Delta\),

\[
U_{\epsilon,\Delta}=C_\epsilon\,e^{-i\Delta T_c|r_0\rangle\langle r_0|}.
\tag{17}
\]

This factorization is exact, including the ramps, because every allowed triad control commutes with the reference projector. The relative phase is \(\arg z+\Delta T_c\). Consequently no control sequence that keeps this reference uncoupled can echo arbitrary unknown \(\Delta\). A known offset can be included in the declared reference phase; residual uncertainty must enter the error budget. Removing it dynamically requires access that exchanges or couples the logical states.

For bright-selective Markov loss into orthogonal absorbing sinks, define the susceptibility \(Q_b(t)\) as the instantaneous bright plane on the arcs and the fixed \(Q\) on the holds, continued through their gap-zero points. The no-jump generator is

\[
\dot K=(-iH-\kappa Q_b/2)K.
\]

Nominal loss contributes \(\kappa X_n/2\) to the primitive's average infidelity and \(3\kappa X_n/2\) to the composite's, to first order in \(\kappa\). Proof: the first no-jump correction is \(-\kappa\int U_0^\dagger Q_bU_0dt/2\); its logical block is \(-\kappa X|d\rangle\langle d|/2\). Averaging the logical overlap gives \(\kappa X/2\). Jumps have zero overlap with the target by the absorbing-sink hypothesis.

For common quasi-static gain \(\epsilon\), independent zero-mean residual reference offset of variance \(\sigma_\Delta^2\), and weak loss, the retained leading terms are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal I_f&\simeq \tfrac52 b_n^2\mathbb E\epsilon^2
+\tfrac12\kappa X_n+\sigma_\Delta^2T_n^2/6,\\
\mathcal I_c&\simeq (\tfrac{15}{2}b_n^2\theta^2+2b_n^4)\mathbb E\epsilon^4
+\tfrac32\kappa X_n+\sigma_\Delta^2T_c^2/6.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]

Mixed terms and higher orders are omitted in (18). For a fixed offset, retain the cross term by using \((2\sqrt3 b_n^2\epsilon^2+\Delta T_c)^2/6\) for the composite's leading phase infidelity. The zero-mean independence in (18) is what removes that cross term.

The composite improves this leading budget exactly when

\[
\boxed{\tfrac52b_n^2\mathbb E\epsilon^2
-(\tfrac{15}{2}b_n^2\theta^2+2b_n^4)\mathbb E\epsilon^4
>\kappa X_n+\frac{\sigma_\Delta^2}{6}(T_c^2-T_n^2).}
\tag{19}
\]

For the +1% example, use the **exact gain-only fidelity difference** in place of the left side of (19). With the other noise source absent, the leading crossovers are \(\kappa/a<2.43984\times10^{-4}\) or \(\sigma_\Delta/a<1.03980\times10^{-3}\). With both present, use their sum in (19). They are weak-noise estimates for this loss law and this reference attachment.
