## RH-1a: the flag condition and its exact obstruction

**[numerical]** All prescribed cases ran. At gain $\epsilon=10^{-2}$ and $10^{-3}$, every trine dump and every forward/inverse FD dump has `DB_k = 1`. Both words take fork **(b)** and exceed the `DB_k > 10^-2 at every dump` kill threshold. The first-click cumulative maps $F_k$ also have rank one. The per-dump singular values, polar completions, matrices and Haar checks are in [rh1a-checks.json](rh1a-checks.json).

**[proved | native gain-only loop, fixed spectator]** For a loop at angle $\alpha$, let $u_\alpha$ be its active code line and $u_\alpha^\perp$ its spectator. The gain-only Hamiltonian annihilates the spectator for the entire loop, including FD ramps and inverse stretches. Therefore

\[
L_k u_\alpha^\perp=u_\alpha^\perp,
\qquad E_k u_\alpha^\perp=0,
\qquad E_k=|\ell_k\rangle\langle u_\alpha|.
\]

**[proved | nonzero leakage]** It follows that

\[
E_k^\dagger E_k=\|\ell_k\|^2 |u_\alpha\rangle\langle u_\alpha|,
\qquad \operatorname{spec}(E_k^\dagger E_k)=\{\|\ell_k\|^2,0\},
\qquad DB_k=1.
\]

**[proved | linear recovery]** No post-click block $C_k$ can increase this rank. For $\psi=a u_\alpha+b u_\alpha^\perp$, the clicked state is $a\ell_k$; the coefficient $b$ is absent. Multiplying by the preceding survivor maps cannot increase rank either: $\operatorname{rank}F_k\le1$. This is an exact obstruction, independently of solver tolerance or the polar-completion choice.

**[proved | resolved measurement record]** The tight-frame sum $\sum F_k^\dagger F_k\approx cI$ constrains the total probability of a click. It does not make any individual $F_k$ an isometry. Forgetting the dump label after it has been measured leaves a sum of Kraus branches, not a coherent sum of amplitudes. A separate coherent accumulation of temporal modes would change the instrument; it was not inserted into this test.

**[numerical]** At 1% gain the original erasures are `0.00159176465538` (trine) and `0.00265153322393` (five). These agree with the existing gain-only FD/SPLIT results. Detuning also has nonzero leakage: pure $\delta_d=10^{-4}$ gives total first-click rates `1.86893e-9` and `1.20519e-8`, respectively. The assertion that detuning “does not leak” must be understood as a leading-error comparison, not an exact identity.

**[numerical]** Detuning-only per-dump DB values span approximately `0.730–1.000`; mixed gain/detuning values are near one. The reported gain/d-site derivative overlaps $\Omega_k$ are `0.740695` for the trine; `0.464586` at each FD forward dump and `0.600919` at each inverse dump. Distinguishability was reported, not made a success requirement.

## RH-1c: a statistical detector, not the predicted translated window

**[numerical]** The seven detunings, two gain settings and both timing dithers ran. $p_N$ is the final *first-click probability per original input*, averaged over known Haar inputs; rates conditioned on reaching that dump are also recorded. Raw $p_N(\delta)$ at 1% gain is not a pure quadratic law: it has a gain baseline and a mixed odd term.

**[numerical]** At 1% gain, $p_N(0)=5.3044733193\times10^{-4}$. The asymmetry at zero detuning is already `9.15466e-6` for $dt=0.02\tau$ and `1.41750e-4` for $dt=0.05\tau$. Its detuning slopes are `-4.50673e-5` and `-6.98053e-4`, respectively. It is a calibrated signed signal, not a line through zero. At zero gain the offsets vanish within numerical error and the slopes are `-4.62957e-5` and `-7.16969e-4`.

**[proved | endpoint Hamiltonian and fixed Q readout]** At the nominal endpoint, $QH(T)P=0$, including the diagonal site detunings. Consequently, for the cumulative bright amplitude $f=F_N\psi$,

\[
\partial_t f=-iH_{QQ}(T)f,
\qquad \partial_t\|f\|^2=0.
\]

**[proved | zero nominal gain/detuning]** There $f=0$, so the first timing tangent $\beta$ itself vanishes. The first nonzero timing amplitude is second order and the rate begins at fourth order. A quadratic-fit vertex cannot be interpreted as a first-order translation without checking its stability and higher-order terms. At nonzero gain, $\beta$ initially rotates the existing bright amplitude without changing its norm.

**[numerical]** At 1% gain, the local complex amplitude alignment is `0.158844`, while the real interference alignment is `-0.110161`. At zero gain the alignment is undefined because the timing tangent is zero. The fit-width test at $\delta=0,\epsilon=0.01$ returns vertices `-0.0839894`, `-0.0345990`, and `-0.000491327` for widths $0.05\tau,0.02\tau,0.002\tau$. Those are fitting artifacts around the stationary endpoint, not a displaced exact-return time. The receipt additionally searches both sides of zero within $[-0.05\tau,0.05\tau]$ and records the achieved reduction without promoting a bounded search to a global timing theorem.

**[numerical]** The bounded search finds no resolved reduction at 1% gain. At zero gain a very shallow minimum does move: at $\delta=\pm10^{-3}$, the selected offsets are approximately `+0.001184` and `-0.001171`, with only `9.14e-8` and `8.55e-8` of the nominal rate removed. ## RH-1d: virtual comparison and physical drive lock

**[numerical]** At the trine point $(0,10^{-4},0)$, conditional error is `2.42028e-8` and its unitary fraction is `0.999999999990`. Raw `Zc = 0.166457`; removing the global phase gives `Zc = 1.9999995`. This is a tilted residual, fork **(b)**.

**[numerical]** All four FD mode points are also overwhelmingly unitary, with fractions above `0.9999957`. The spec's raw metric gives values `9.05e-5–2.63e-3` and hence formally selects fork (a). After global-phase removal the values are `1.948–2.000`: the actual small logical rotations are tilted, not pure Z. The raw outcomes and corrected values are both retained in [rh1d-checks.json](rh1d-checks.json). In particular, the pure-common prediction of a substantial nonunitary component is false at this interface and scale.

**[proved | definition of Zc]** A scalar phase on $W$ leaves the commutator norm unchanged but changes $\|W-I\|$. An unobservable common phase can therefore dominate the denominator and hide a tilted small rotation. The corrected diagnostic uses $W'=e^{-i\arg\operatorname{tr}W}W$, locally near the identity. A rotated final readout removes the polar unitary for that comparison; implementing subsequent words in that frame still requires a compatible control/compilation construction.

**[proved | declared Schrödinger equation]** Put $R(t)=e^{s i\widehat\delta t D}$, where $s=\pm1$, and drive $H(t)=R H_0R^\dagger+\delta D$. For $U=RV$,

\[
i\dot V=[H_0+(\delta+s\widehat\delta)D]V.
\]

**[numerical]** The literal positive-phase differential prescription doubles the residual at equal rates; it does not give nominal evolution. The negative-phase prescription cancels it. The independent laboratory integration verifies $R^\dagger U=U_{\rm residual}$ within `1e-12` and restores the complete sampled nominal dither profile. It does not claim $U=U_0$ in the laboratory frame. The remaining known endpoint phase must be included in the readout/target convention.

**[proved | generator normalization + numerical]** For common mode, $\delta_d=\delta_r=\delta$ means the noise is $\delta P$. When the lock generator is $P/2$, exact cancellation needs $\widehat\delta=2\delta$. This correctly normalized negative-phase lock restores the nominal erasure/window profile too. Therefore the “common-mode lock cannot reach a gap error” prediction is false for this explicitly enlarged complex drive family. It is not a statement about availability of those controls in hardware.

**[numerical]** At zero gain, eight residual-rate samples around the matched lock give conditional-error powers **1.99999996** (differential) and **3.99999906** (common). The latter is quartic in this trine channel. At 1% gain, exact lock returns to the existing gain-only conditional floor and erasure profile; it does not repair the rank-one clicked branches.

**[declared model extension]** The couplings are complex and Hermitian, $R H_0R^\dagger$, rather than the original real-symmetric RF-7 family. The exact-match result is a gauge/control identity and an implementation check. It assumes a supplied detuning estimate and phase reference; RH-1 does not close the sensing/feedback loop or demonstrate stochastic tracking.
