## Native angle correction

**[numerical diagnosis]** After removing global phase, the trine's polar residual at zero gain and d-site detuning `1e-4` has Pauli-axis direction approximately `(X,Y,Z)=(-1.87e-13, 0.999999753, -0.000702529)`, with rotation parameter `0.000190536522`. Its leading residual is the real code-plane rotation generated by Y. The existing `rotation(alpha)` supplies that control direction.

**[proved | nominal reflection geometry]** For `R(a)=exp(-i a Y)`, the reflection satisfies `G(a)=R(a) G R(a)^T=R(2a)G`. Adding a common shift `s` to the three trine angles shifts the nominal reflection axis by `s`. This motivates `s=-theta/2` for a calibrated leading Y residual. The laboratory site detuning and fidelity target remain fixed; this is a physical control change.

## A gimballed angle: feedback feasibility

**[user proposal, 6 September 2026]** Replace choosing one good angle with adjusting the orientation to track the low-error region. A concrete active version uses the existing common-angle actuator, a fixed-target signed probe, and one retained controller coordinate `s`. This is an added measurement/control law. Neither the existing geometry nor the DIS storage programme by itself supplies a sensor or a passive restoring force.

**[derived | native reflection geometry, locally dominant Y residual]** Command `alpha_j(n)=alpha_j(0)+s_n` for every loop in a word. Hold `s_n` fixed throughout the complete word. Write its leading output action as `R(e_n) G`, with `e_n ≈ Theta_n+2s_n`, where `Theta_n` is the unknown residual at zero command. The target `G`, laboratory detuning and readout basis remain fixed. All relative trine angles are preserved. The single angle controls this leading component; it is not a controller for arbitrary residual SU(2) errors.

**[declared sensor extension]** On reference trials, prepare known `|d>` and `|r>` inputs and measure X in the accepted code output. Let their normalized means be `m_d,m_r` and use `m=(m_d-m_r)/2`. For the leading unitary above, `m=sin(2e)≈2e`; its sign identifies the correction direction. The actual probe runner uses the full nonunitary surviving map, counts erasures separately, and uses the normalized output probabilities to generate binomial measurement records. It assumes ideal reference preparation and code-X readout; their native physical implementation is not provided. Unknown computational data are not these probe trials. The reference must experience the same drift as the subsequent data words.

**[derived | local response, negligible additional latency]** The update is

\[
s_{n+1}=s_n-\frac{g}{4}\widehat m_n,
\qquad
e_{n+1}\simeq(1-g)e_n+\Delta\Theta_n-\frac g2\eta_n,
\]

where `m_hat=2e+eta`. At fixed detuning, the linearized noiseless loop is stable for `0<g<2`. This is a local acquisition statement, not global convergence of the periodic fringe. The controller need not identify the detuning or know the best angle: it only needs the signed residual. For constant angle drift `v=Delta Theta` per epoch, the local steady lag is `v/g`.

**[exploratory closed-loop probe]** Run `python experiments/2026-09-06/rh1_gimbal_probe.py --json docs/receipts/rh1-gimbal-probe.json`. With unreported-to-controller gain error `0.01`, the plant detuning takes values `+1e-4`, `-1e-4`, `+1.2e-4`, each for 20 calibration epochs. The controller starts at zero, uses `g=0.5`, and can see only its previous command and the paired X measurement record. Metrics are evaluated before that epoch's update, so they include one epoch of response delay. Comparisons retain the original fixed calibrated angle and a stronger fixed angle fitted with knowledge of all three future plateaus. The latter uses bounded scalar minimization over `[-0.001,0.001]`; this is a local numerical fit, not a global optimality certificate. Eight checks pass: four local slope checks, three instrument completeness checks, and ideal-readout reacquisition. The measured command slopes range from `3.99985430` to `4.00014934`, including both detuning signs and gains 0/0.01.

- With exact expected readouts, final conditional errors for the three plateaus are `7.39e-13`, `7.28e-13`, `1.11e-12`. This ideal sensor calculation demonstrates the actuator and feedback sign, not finite measurement precision.
- With `1e8` attempted reference shots per epoch, the single seeded trajectory has mean settled conditional error `5.36e-10`, versus `3.29e-8` for the original fixed angle and `2.41e-8` for the hindsight-fitted fixed angle on the same epochs. This is about a 45-fold conditional improvement over the stronger comparison in that trace, with six billion attempted reference shots in total.
- With `1e6` attempted shots per epoch, the same controller has mean settled error `4.45e-8`: measurement noise makes this trace worse than the fixed-angle comparison. The controller has not been retuned after seeing that failure.

“Settled” means the last eight epochs of each 20-epoch plateau, a descriptive window rather than a preregistered score. Both finite-shot cases are single seeded traces, not ensemble confidence intervals. The maximum erasure remains about `0.001593` in all three runs; angle tracking does not repair that loss. Complete raw trajectories, counts, assumptions and source hashes are in [the receipt](../../../../receipts/rh1-gimbal-probe.json).

**[derived measurement cost | independent probes, local unitary limit]** For `N_acc` total accepted probes, split equally between the two inputs, `Var(m_hat)≈1/N_acc`. At constant detuning,

\[
\operatorname{Var}(e)\simeq\frac{g}{4(2-g)N_{\rm acc}},
\qquad
\mathbb E[r_{\rm conditional}]\simeq
\frac{g}{6(2-g)N_{\rm acc}}.
\]

At `g=0.5` the measurement contribution is approximately `1/(18 N_acc)`, before remaining-axis errors and drift lag. More averaging reduces that noise but lengthens the calibration epoch. The serial native word time alone is `N*9*TAU`, or `5.4753e9` inverse-coupling time units per `1e8`-shot epoch. The simulation holds detuning constant throughout each batch, omits preparation/readout time and decoherence, and imposes the changes by epoch rather than a measured physical drift spectrum. It therefore does not establish a useful hardware tracking bandwidth.

**[derived continuous-motion boundary]** For a continuously moving embedded code frame `R(s(t))`, transforming `H(t)=R H_0 R†+D` gives `H_rot=H_0+R† D R-dot(s)Y_P`. The connection term follows from `-i R† dot R`; it changes the within-word dynamics. A constantly moving gimbal needs a new propagator/cancellation analysis. Holding the command between complete words avoids inserting that motion into the existing word proof, while actuator transitions and their cost remain to be modeled.

**[assessment and next falsifier]** Active common-angle tracking has a concrete local law and survives a finite-count model at sufficiently high shot cost. The next decisive target is a physically available, more informative signed reference measurement with a measured latency and drift bandwidth; compare against the fixed angle at matched total time and include erasure, transient error and reference overhead. A true passive or coherent autonomous gimbal would additionally need a derived coupling and restoring mechanism. No such mechanism is established by the feedback probe.

**[provenance]** Existing native reflection and RH-1 instrument sources supply the plant. The predictive-state construction makes retention of the command and evolving environment explicit; it does not determine this stochastic controller. Measurement-based qubit drift feedback is established background: [Vepsäläinen et al., Improving qubit coherence using closed-loop feedback](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-29287-4), Results and equations (1)–(2). The particular angle law, discriminator, local noise/lag calculation and numerical results above are derived for this native word. See EXT-023 and SR-011.

## Coherent collection across dumps

**[proved | proposed coherent temporal register]** Stack the existing cumulative first-click maps into `F_coh = [F_1; ...; F_N]`. Then `F_coh† F_coh = sum F_k† F_k = I - K† K`. If this is scalar, the entire collected map is a scaled isometry even though each individual dump map has rank one. Total near-blindness therefore has a potential use when all temporal amplitudes remain coherently accessible. A later presence/absence measurement must preserve coherence among those modes. Discarding a timestamp after detection does not achieve that.

**[exploratory ideal algebra]** At gain `0.01`, take rectangular polars `F_coh=U H`, `K=V J`, and recovery `C=V U†`. The complete channel with Kraus maps `{K, C F_coh}` has computed average infidelity `1.55357e-14` for the trine and `1.03069e-14` for the five-loop word. Trace-preservation defects are below `1e-15`. This assumes a coherent register; finite capture hardware has not been simulated.

**[exploratory robustness falsifier]** Fix the same calibration. At another gain, the leaked image can leave the calibrated range of U. Retain that failure explicitly as `(I-U U†)F_coh`, mapped to `I/2` for a trace-preserving diagnostic. At gain `0.005`, recovered errors are `1.32728e-7` (trine) and `5.99775e-7` (five). At gain `0.02`, they are `8.13807e-6` and `3.69082e-5`. The favorable calibrated result does not establish a gain-independent compressor.

**[proposed next falsifier]** Construct admissible coherent capture/return controls and derive error-family correction conditions across a gain interval, including relative mode phases, memory/control cost and every failure branch. A fixed recovery must work on a neighborhood. This is a new instrument; RH-1's measured per-dump kill is unchanged.
